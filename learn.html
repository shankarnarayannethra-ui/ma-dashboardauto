M&A BASICS

Learn the language of deals.

Clear definitions for readers who want the news without unnecessary financial jargon.

Merger

Two companies combine into one organization, often presented as a partnership between similarly positioned businesses.

Acquisition

One company purchases control of another company, its shares, or selected assets.

Deal premium

The amount offered above the target’s unaffected market price to encourage shareholder approval.

Synergy

Expected value from combining operations, such as lower costs, higher revenue, or stronger capabilities.

Enterprise value

A measure of a company’s total operating value that includes equity and debt, less cash.

Goodwill

The accounting value recorded when the purchase price exceeds the fair value of identifiable net assets.

Why the two stocks often move differently

The acquirer’s stock may dip because investors worry about the premium, debt, integration risk, or overpayment. The target’s stock often rises toward the offer price because shareholders are being offered more than the prior market value. This is a tendency, not a rule.
